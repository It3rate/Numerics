Emergent Properties

There is nothing like experience. We can debate all day whether machines reason, have emergent characteristics, or come up with novel ideas -- but at the heart of it, they don't have experiences. Without experience, the best they can do is simulation.

It is very informative then, to create a simple environment where a machine can have an experience. First, it forces you to commit to what an experience is, exactly. Second, you soon realize that an experience requires the sensing of it, an interpretation, and a selected reaction. If a tree falls on a rock, the rock didn't experience anything.

So imagine a line making machine. All it can do is move forward and turn. This could be a living thing, but let's not over complicating things just yet. All that exists in this world is a blank space, and a line that moves left or right. The only 'experience' here is perhaps whatever it feels when it moves, but let's imagine it feels nothing. There is no feedback, so there is nothing to interpret, and nothing to react to. It wouldn't even be able to tell if it was moving in spite of leaving a trailing line.

Next, if we add some obstacles to the environment. This doesn't add any capabilities to the line machine, it tries to move forward, but it can't. It can still turn though, and eventually will get around the obstacles. This does add the potential to interpret and decide. Before there were no external things to interpret, now there are. With enough capability, it could even start to map out the world. The key here, is there are new capabilities, but no 'emergent' properties. The obstacles exist in this world with or without it, it's only learning about them. The line machine isn't creating new information, or leaving the world more complex than it found it.

Now look at the line intersecting itself. This isn't terribly different than hitting an obstacle, but for one gigantically important thing. It is experiencing an artifact of itself existing in the world. It needs to interpret something that wouldn't have otherwise existed, a thing that isn't built up of the primitives that make up the world it is in. If you pause and thing, this means intelligence can't just be layers of scaffolding built up on top of the smallest bits of sensory input. Intelligence needs to be constantly scanning for new patterns of information that *did not exist* in the previous world-level, in any form.

You can imagine this line could creating enclosures, angles, density changes, repeating patterns, textures and many other 'concepts'. But in the world without line machines, there is no possible concept of path density. And in the other direction, there is no concept of these textured paths creating a letter that represents a sound, or a line sketch of an old man's house in the woods. As you add more line machines, you could see them clustering and mutating into even more complex machines that make the world even more complex to interpret. These would again require more intelligence to experience it, as it will have things that weren't even conceptually possible in the previous level. You could imagine this, or just look outside and see it.

The takeaway is, intelligence is not only understanding our environment by building up from some useful base primitives. It is constantly scanning for new emergent patterns in an ever changing landscape -- a landscape that intelligence is able to shape and reinterpret. Itellignence is experiencing the world, and the world is experiencing it.
